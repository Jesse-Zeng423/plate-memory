# Practical review — October 3, 2026

The product helps a hungry friend find a food direction without an account, menu
import or model setup. It is not a complete ordering service or health adviser.

## What the current flow actually does

- Exact English/Chinese food keywords return paged, attributed records from the
  3,535-item offline pack and matching historical personal choices.
- Loose recognized cravings such as “something warm” or “I want noodles” return
  explicitly labeled nearby ideas. Editorial groups use original source categories.
  Constraints are not relaxed into false clearance, affordability or nutrient claims.
- Details show the published food family and editorial questions to check on the
  actual menu. Related ideas are selectable, with Back returning to the first list.
- Selection leads to a delivery-app/cafeteria-board next step, never an order or a
  recorded meal. No current shop inventory, price, portion or ingredients are known.
- Actual menu review uses existing local AI candidate extraction and unchanged
  deterministic memory guards. Name-only discovery has no dietary clearance.
- Optional private journal and friend-authored notes complete a companionship loop.
  Explicit postcard exports work in both demo and normal mode, without recording
  synthetic meals or dietary profiles.

## What still limits daily usefulness

A source catalog contains many variants and cannot establish what Harold can buy
on his campus. Loose-craving recognition is bounded and editorial; it cannot
interpret every sentence. Published category names are retained even where no
Chinese translation is supplied. No verified nutrient values or health ranking
are shipped. Tests and synthetic replay do not establish real adoption.

The highest-value next evidence is a real hungry first-use trial. Ask which ideas
he could actually buy and where he hesitated. With permission, save a small set of
his real campus/delivery choices privately with dated prices and his own notes.
That makes future literal search more actionable without a remote service. Actual
menus can be supplied locally for existing ingredient review; a future local menu
snapshot importer could link choices to dated menu evidence. Those extensions
should follow real feedback rather than invent Harold’s preferences or availability.
